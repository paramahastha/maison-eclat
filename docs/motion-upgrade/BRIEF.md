# Motion upgrade brief

User request (2026-11-07): explore Chanel's Bleu fragrance experience and D&G Velvet layering, then make Maison Éclat more cinematic and interactive **as the visitor scrolls**. Preserve original branding/assets, routes, native scrolling and accessible fallbacks. Firefox remains excluded by request.

## Direction

Retain the dark-room art direction. Tell a sequence through the object: encounter its silhouette, inspect its form, open the composition, discover three identities, then return to a chosen signature. Scroll drives the bottle, lighting, note motifs, gallery depth and editorial framing. No reference-brand assets, source code or product designs enter the application.

## Implemented sequence

1. Encounter: a finite desktop sticky stage provides a reversible 81-degree bottle turn, a gentle approach/recession, moving highlight and progress line.
2. Origin: an ivory study reveals the bottle's side and lifts the cap slightly as the visual aperture opens.
3. Composition: the cap separates farther, the bottle turns, and botanical lines and note orbits move at different rates. Top/heart/base controls additionally change pose and emphasis.
4. Collection: three original bottles form a spatial gallery. Desktop scroll rotates the arrangement through Obsidian, Neroli and Santal; palette, label, narrative and link follow. Drag, arrows and keyboard controls use the same selection and timeline.
5. Ritual: the original macro image pans beneath the editorial text with a counter-moving text layer.
6. Invitation: the selected bottle returns and settles toward the final product destination.

Two held scenes only, native document scrolling throughout. Compact/short viewports use natural-flow chapters with motion; reduced motion uses complete static artwork and immediate controls. The user clarified scroll as the priority, so the earlier proposed pairing studio and additional product controls were removed from this upgrade's scope.
